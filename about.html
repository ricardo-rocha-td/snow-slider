About

Hi, I'm Ricardo. I've been a software developer for eight years and a gamer for even longer, so I finally decided to try making a game myself. Snow Slider is the result, built in my free time using Godot, with everything from the code and levels to the art and sounds (every sound effect is generated in code!).

I've always liked games that make you think and figure things out, whether it's solving a puzzle that takes a while to click, building empires, planning strategies or exploring ancient ruins. I like the challenge of figuring things out and discovering new places. When I started Snow Slider, though, I wanted to keep things simple. The idea was to make a puzzle game where, once you start sliding on ice, you can't stop until something gets in your way. If you've ever slipped on ice, you know how that goes. From there, I kept adding new mechanics, obstacles and things to experiment with, until somehow I ended up with 13 mountains, different worlds to explore, a bit of sci-fi and a penguin with an ever-growing collection of hats.

While I built Snow Slider myself, I didn't do it alone. Friends and family have helped test levels, shared ideas and spotted things I completely missed. I've also discovered how frustrating it can be to watch someone play your game and completely ignore a rule you thought was obvious.

Karla, my girlfriend, has been a big part of the game from the beginning. She's played pretty much every version, tested new levels, reviewed descriptions and helped come up with ideas like hidden Easter eggs and daily challenges. As someone who usually prefers story-driven games and murder mysteries, she's a tough critic, and we don't always agree. But she's shaped more of the game than anyone else, and quite a few levels wouldn't be the same without her.

We hope Snow Slider is something you can pick up whenever you need a break, whether you're on a crowded bus, waiting at the doctor's office or just tired of scrolling through social media and reading bad news. Something fun that makes you think, lets you explore a little and gives you that satisfying feeling when you finally figure out a puzzle.

Thanks for playing! If you find a bug, have an idea or want to share a clever solution, let us know.

P.S. A note from Karla: I'm obviously biased, but one of my favourite things about Snow Slider is how much you can discover in these tiny worlds. Don't just rush through the puzzles! Look around, explore and keep an eye out for... well, surprises. There are quite a few hidden throughout the game. If you find the bear under the mountain, try clicking it and see what happens. Have fun!
