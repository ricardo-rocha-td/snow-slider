Snow Slider Puzzles: Support

Slide a penguin across the ice and find the way to the flag.

Found a bug, stuck on a level, or have an idea? Write to snowsliderpuzzles@gmail.com. Please say which phone you have and, for a level, which mountain and level number it is.

How do I play?

Swipe to slide. The penguin keeps going until something stops it, so plan where each slide ends. Reach the flag to finish the level. Finish in as few moves as the best way (par) for three stars.

I'm stuck on a level

Tap the lightbulb for a hint: it shows the next moves from wherever the penguin is, and never costs stars. You get hints for completing levels. You can also undo a slide (once per try) or restart. The book button explains every object you've found.

How do I open the next mountain?

Each mountain opens once you've earned enough stars on the one before it. Going back to improve old levels to three stars counts too. A few crossings on the way also need stars in total: the gondola runs at 150 ★ and the Wall's gate opens at 250 ★, and the map shows how many you have. The last mountain asks for more still.

Where are my hats?

Tap the penguin button on the mountain map. Hats are earned with stars across every mountain, never bought.

Is my progress saved? Can I move it to a new phone?

Progress is saved automatically after every level. On iPhone and iPad it's also kept in your iCloud, so it comes back if you delete and reinstall the game, and it's shared by your iPhone and iPad when they're signed in to the same Apple Account (with iCloud on). On Android it's kept on that phone only, and goes if you delete the game.

Can I turn off the snow or the moving effects?

Yes, in the settings (the gear on the title screen or the map). "Weather" turns off the falling snow and the storms, and "Camera shake" the shake when the penguin hits an ice spike. Under Accessibility, "Reduce motion" holds still the things that only move for decoration (smoke, waving flags, sparkles and the like), and "Colour-blind mode" gives matching portals, plates and gates their own shapes, so they can be told apart without colour.
